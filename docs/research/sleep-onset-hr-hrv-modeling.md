# Sleep-Onset Physiology, HR/HRV Modeling, PPG Filtering, and Online Per-User Calibration

> **Scope:** Primary-source synthesis for `umutaktepe/BPMute` (Issue [#2](https://github.com/umutaktepe/BPMute/issues/2)). Evaluates peer-reviewed sleep physiology, wearable photoplethysmography (PPG) validation, signal processing standards, change-point/state-space detection algorithms, and robust online calibration methods for detecting wake-to-sleep onset from wrist-worn heart rate streams (both `BPM + RR-interval` and `BPM-only`).

## Executive Summary

1. **Physiology of sleep onset (Quiet Wake → N1 → N2):** Transitioning from quiet wakefulness into NREM sleep produces a coordinated **withdrawal of sympathetic tone** and **relative shift toward parasympathetic (vagal) predominance** ([Somers et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199302043280502); [Trinder et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2869.2001.00263.x); [de Zambotti et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.03.027)). Heart rate decelerates progressively before and across sleep onset (~0.52 bpm/min in the 5 minutes pre-onset, continuing more gradually post-onset; [de Zambotti et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz312)), reaching ~5 bpm or more below quiet wakefulness in consolidated NREM ([Somers et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199302043280502)). Critically, **overall HRV (SDNN, VLF, and LF power) decreases** across the transition (VLF drops ~2 minutes prior to onset) as slow sympathetic fluctuations vanish, while **vagal HF/RMSSD remains preserved or rises relative to LF**, driving down the **LF/HF ratio** and collapsing rolling HR variance ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006)).
2. **PPG artifact & dropout filtering:** Wrist PPG motion artifacts span 0.1–10 Hz—overlapping the entire cardiac band—and cannot be removed by linear filtering alone ([Charlton et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/acead2)). Pulse rate variability (PRV) is an acceptable surrogate for ECG-derived HRV only during quiet rest/sleep ([Schäfer & Vagedes, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcard.2012.03.119)). A safe real-time pipeline layers: (a) BLE Heart Rate Service (`0x2A37`) sensor-contact flags and zero/stale packet gating ([Bluetooth SIG HRS 1.0](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/heart-rate-service-1-0/)), (b) hard physiological bounds, (c) causal quartile-based beat classification (`dRR`/`mRR`, [Lipponen & Tarvainen, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/03091902.2019.1640306)) for RR streams and Hampel median/MAD filtering ([Pearson, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/87.974338)) for BPM-only streams, and (d) Signal Quality Index (SQI) gating that freezes accumulators during dropouts/motion.
3. **Detection architecture (high wake specificity first):** Across consumer wearables validated against polysomnography (PSG), sleep sensitivity is high (≥93%) while **wake specificity is chronically low (18–60%)** because stillness mimics sleep ([Walch et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz180); [Chinoy et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsaa291)). For an auto-mute trigger where false positives (muting while awake) are far costlier than a 2–5 min delay, a **multi-stage state machine** wrapping **one-sided lower CUSUM** ([Page, 1954](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100)) or a **3-state forward-filtered HMM** ([Rabiner, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.18626); [Kortelainen et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITB.2010.2044797)) over multi-feature emissions (relative HR drop, negative slope, rolling HR variance collapse, and—when RR is available—$\ln\text{RMSSD}$ + RMSSD/SDNN stability) with explicit warmup and confirmation hysteresis delivers strong specificity and seamless degradation from `BPM+RR` to `BPM-only`.
4. **Cold-start & safe online calibration:** Population resting HR spans 40–109 bpm while within-individual daily SD is only **3.03 bpm** ([Quer et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0227709)), and nocturnal RMSSD varies dramatically across demographics ([Natarajan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(20)30246-6)). Fixed absolute BPM/HRV cutoffs are therefore unviable. Cold start must rely purely on **within-session relative shifts**, then converge toward user-specific priors via **Empirical Bayes conjugate shrinkage** ([Gelman et al., 2013](#references)) guarded by **median/MAD outlier-night rejection** ([Leys et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.013)) and **Huber-bounded updates** ([Huber, 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732)) to prevent acute stressors—such as illness (+6% HR, −10–12% RMSSD) or alcohol (−12% RMSSD) ([Altini & Plews, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21237932))—from corrupting the model.

## 1. Cardiac Autonomic Physiology: Quiet Wakefulness vs. Wake-to-Sleep Transition

### 1.1 Autonomic Shift Across the Sleep Onset Period

Sleep onset is not an instantaneous binary switch; it is a graded neuro-autonomic transition from relaxed wakefulness through Stage N1 into consolidated Stage N2 NREM sleep ([Carrington et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00702.2004); [de Zambotti et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.03.027)). Primary microneurography and polysomnography studies establish the following sequence:

- **Pre-sleep quiet wakefulness ("lights out" to N1):** Simply lying supine and relaxing reduces postural sympathetic load, but wakefulness retains irregular breathing, cortical fluctuations, and tonic sympathetic activity. Even before clear EEG theta activity (Stage N1) emerges, heart rate and blood pressure begin decelerating ([Carrington et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00702.2004); [Burgess et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2869.1999.00149.x)).
- **Across sleep onset (N1 → N2):**
  - **Heart rate deceleration rate and magnitude:** In a longitudinal PSG analysis of the falling-asleep transition, heart rate dropped at a mean rate of **~0.52 bpm/min during the 5 minutes preceding sleep onset** (~2.6 bpm across the immediate pre-onset window) and continued falling at a slower rate during the 5 minutes immediately following onset ([de Zambotti et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz312)). Across the progression from quiet wakefulness into consolidated NREM sleep, direct physiological recordings show mean HR declining from **64 ± 2 bpm in wakefulness to 59 ± 2 bpm in deep NREM** (~5 bpm / ~8% drop) alongside a **59% reduction in muscle sympathetic-nerve activity amplitude** (from 100% in wake to 41% in Stage 4 NREM; [Somers et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199302043280502)).
  - **Staged N1 → N2 stabilization:** Stepwise reductions in HR occur from lights-out through N1 and into stable N2, where HR plateaus at a lower, low-variance autonomic set-point once spontaneous microarousals subside ([Carrington et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00702.2004); [Trinder et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2869.2001.00263.x)).
  - **Microarousals and K-complexes:** During N1 and N2, cortical microarousals and EEG K-complexes trigger transient bursts of sympathetic nerve activity, brief blood pressure surges, and abrupt HR accelerations that delay or temporarily reverse sleep-onset deceleration ([Somers et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199302043280502); [Carrington et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00702.2004); [Carrington & Trinder, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18457234/)).
  - **Insomnia / hyperarousal caveat:** Individuals with sleep-onset insomnia frequently exhibit persistent sympathetic hyperarousal (blunted cardiac down-regulation during pre-sleep wakefulness), producing shallower or delayed HR drops at onset ([de Zambotti et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.03.027)).
  - **REM sleep contrast (post-onset context):** Later in the night (typically 70–120 min post-onset), REM sleep produces profound sympathetic surges exceeding wakefulness, returning HR and HRV variability to wake-like profiles ([Somers et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199302043280502); [Chouchou & Desseilles, 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4263095/); [Tobaldini et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2013.00294)). Because healthy sleep initiates via NREM (N1/N2), REM rarely confounds initial onset, though it matters if post-trigger re-checking is performed.
  - **Circadian overlay:** Both waking and NREM HR and HF power exhibit strong circadian rhythmicity independent of sleep stage, with maximal vagal predominance occurring around ~02:00 and lower baseline HR during the biological night compared to daytime naps ([Boudreau et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.3230)).

### 1.2 Behavior of HR, Variance, Slope, and HRV Metrics Across States

A critical subtlety from time-frequency PSG analysis ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006)) is that **total variability (SDNN / total power) does not rise monotonically at sleep onset—it typically drops**, because slow sympathetic/behavioral oscillations (VLF and LF) dissipate before and during onset:

- **VLF power (≤0.04 Hz)** begins decreasing significantly **~2 minutes before** EEG-defined sleep onset ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006)).
- **LF power (0.04–0.15 Hz)** decreases across the wake-to-NREM transition, reflecting sympathetic withdrawal and baroreflex stabilization ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006); [Trinder et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2869.2001.00263.x)).
- **HF power (0.15–0.40 Hz) and RMSSD** reflect rapid vagally mediated respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA). Right at N1 transition, HF often stays flat or rises modestly while LF drops, then rises further as N2/N3 consolidates ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006); [Chouchou & Desseilles, 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4263095/); [Tobaldini et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2013.00294)).
- **LF/HF ratio** drops reliably across the wake → N1 → N2 transition because the denominator (HF) holds or rises while the numerator (LF) falls ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006); [Boudreau et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.3230)).
- **Rolling HR variance / local SD of BPM** drops markedly once stable N2 is entered, because behavioral micro-movements, cognitive arousal fluctuations, and sighs disappear—providing a **strong BPM-only proxy** for VLF/LF withdrawal ([Walch et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz180); [Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006)).

| Feature / Metric | Quiet Wakefulness (Supine) | Wake → N1 Transition | Stable N2 Sleep | Microarousal / Movement | Engineering Implication for BPMute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heart Rate (BPM)** | Baseline quiet-wake set-point; episodic drifts | Negative slope (~−0.5 bpm/min pre-onset) | Sustained lower plateau (~4–8% / ~3–7 bpm below quiet wake) | Abrupt transient spike (+5–15 bpm) | Primary signal in both `BPM+RR` and `BPM-only` modes; must be normalized per-session & per-user |
| **HR Slope (3–5 min)** | Near-zero or fluctuating | Consistently negative | Flattens near zero at low plateau | Positive spike | Useful transition cue during N1 entry; cannot be sole trigger (plateau in N2 has zero slope) |
| **Rolling HR Variance / Local SD (BPM)** | Moderate-to-high (sighs, thoughts, minor shifts) | Declining as VLF/LF oscillations subside | Low and steady (only smooth RSA oscillation) | Sharp variance burst | High-value **BPM-only** discriminator between quiet wakefulness and consolidated N2 |
| **RMSSD / pNN50 (Vagal HRV)** | Moderate baseline (user-dependent) | Stable or rising relative to SDNN | Preserved / elevated (especially in consolidated N2/N3) | Drops sharply during sympathetic burst | Best ultra-short-term RR feature; track $\ln\text{RMSSD}$ to normalize right-skewed distribution |
| **SDNN (Total HRV)** | Moderate-to-high (includes VLF/LF drift) | **Decreases** as slow drifts subside | Lower than active wake; dominated by HF | Spikes sharply | Use **RMSSD / SDNN ratio** (high-freq fraction) rather than raw SDNN alone |

| **LF/HF Ratio** | Higher (sympathovagal balance) | Decreases across onset | Low (parasympathetic predominance) | Surges | Strong discriminator when clean ≥2–5 min RR stream exists, fragile under PPG noise |

### 1.3 Minimum Window Lengths for Real-Time HRV Estimation

Clinical HRV standards define **5-minute recordings** as the standard for short-term frequency-domain analysis (VLF ≤0.04 Hz, LF 0.04–0.15 Hz, HF 0.15–0.40 Hz; [Task Force of the ESC & NASPE, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.93.5.1043)), and caution that ultra-short (<5 min), short-term (5 min), and 24-hour norms are not directly interchangeable ([Shaffer & Ginsberg, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2017.00258)).

For real-time sleep-onset tracking where 5-minute latency is too sluggish for responsive feature updates, [Munoz et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0138921) validated ultra-short time-domain windows against gold-standard 240–300 s recordings:
- **RMSSD** achieves $r \approx 0.853\text{–}0.862$ in a single **10 s** strip, $r \approx 0.941$ when averaging three 10 s strips, and **$r = 0.986$ at 120 s**.
- **SDNN** is less reliable in 10 s ($r \approx 0.758\text{–}0.764$), becomes acceptable at **30–60 s**, and reaches **$r = 0.956$ at 120 s**.
- **Design takeaway:** Compute **$\ln\text{RMSSD}$** over rolling **60 s** windows (updated every 10–15 s) and **SDNN / rolling HR variance** over **60–120 s** windows. Avoid relying on FFT/Lomb-Scargle LF/HF in windows shorter than 120–180 s because spectral leakage and even a few missing wrist-PPG beats severely distort LF/HF estimates.

## 2. Artifact, Outlier, and Contact-Dropout Filtering for Wrist PPG Streams

### 2.1 Wrist PPG Failure Modes and PRV-vs-HRV Constraints

Wrist-worn optical sensors measure **photoplethysmography (PPG)**—volumetric blood-flow pulses—rather than cardiac electrical R-waves (ECG). Two physical realities govern signal processing:

1. **Motion artifact spectral overlap:** Physical motion, tendon tension, and strap displacement generate PPG noise across **0.1–10 Hz**, overlapping directly with both the fundamental heart-rate band (0.5–3.5 Hz) and respiratory/HRV modulation bands ([Charlton et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/acead2)). Consequently, simple linear low-pass or band-pass filtering cannot eliminate motion artifacts; signal quality indexing (SQI) and non-linear outlier rejection are mandatory.
2. **PRV as a surrogate for HRV:** A systematic review of PPG-vs-ECG studies concluded that **pulse rate variability (PRV) provides sufficient accuracy as an estimate of HRV during rest and sleep**, though short-term variability can be slightly overestimated due to pulse transit time and respiration-cardiovascular coupling ([Schäfer & Vagedes, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcard.2012.03.119)). During physical movement or acute stress, PRV agreement degrades unacceptably—meaning HRV features must be **gated off during motion or low-SQI windows**.

### 2.2 BLE Heart Rate Service (`0x180D` / `0x2A37`) Framing & Dropout Handling

When consuming standard Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Heart Rate Measurement notifications (characteristic `0x2A37`, [Bluetooth SIG Heart Rate Service 1.0](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/heart-rate-service-1-0/)):

- **Flags Byte (Byte 0):**
  - `Bit 0`: Heart Rate format (`0` = `uint8`, `1` = `uint16`).
  - `Bits 1–2` (`(flags >> 1) & 0b11`): **Sensor Contact Status** — `0b00` or `0b01` = feature unsupported; **`0b10` (`2`) = supported, skin contact NOT detected**; **`0b11` (`3`) = supported, skin contact detected**.
  - `Bit 4`: **RR-Interval Present** (`1` = one or more `uint16` RR intervals appended).
- **RR-Interval Encoding:** Little-endian `uint16` values with resolution **`1/1024` s** ($\approx 0.9766\text{ ms}$): $\text{RR}_{\text{ms}} = (\text{raw} / 1024) \times 1000$. Multiple beats between notifications (~1 Hz) are packed sequentially from **oldest to newest**.
- **Contact-Dropout & Stall Rules:**
  - Any packet with `contactStatus == 0b10`, `BPM == 0`, or packet-arrival gap $> 5\text{ s}$ must immediately emit a `MISSING/DROPOUT` sample—**never** substitute `0` or forward-fill indefinitely, which would falsely mimic bradycardia or zero-variance sleep.
  - Detect **firmware-frozen BPM** (common on wrist watches when optical lock is lost without clearing the contact bit): if raw BPM is bit-identical across $> 20\text{–}30\text{ s}$ with zero RR variation, flag as low-SQI/stale and pause detector accumulation.

### 2.3 Layered Real-Time Filtering Pipeline

Primary HRV and time-series literature supports a four-stage causal filtering pipeline:

| Stage | Applies To | Algorithm / Criterion | Primary Reference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Physiological Bounds Gate** | BPM & RR | Reject $\text{RR} < 300\text{ ms}$ (>200 bpm) or $\text{RR} > 2000\text{ ms}$ (<30 bpm); reject $\text{BPM} \notin [30, 200]$. Prevents division-by-zero and gross sensor glitches. | Clinical resting bounds; [Task Force, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.93.5.1043) |
| **2. Beat-to-Beat Jump & Artifact Classification** | RR-intervals | **Lipponen & Tarvainen (2019) adaptive method:** compute successive differences $dRR_j = RR_j - RR_{j-1}$ and local median deviation $mRR_j = RR_j - \text{median}_{11}(RR)$. Compute adaptive quartile-deviation threshold $Th_1(j) = 5.2 \cdot \text{QD}(dRR)$ over a local beat window (covering 99.95% under normality; adapt centered 91-beat window to a **causal trailing 60–91 beat window** for real-time streaming). Classify missed beats ($\approx 2\times\text{median RR}$, split into two), extra beats (sum adjacent short beats), and ectopic beats (interpolate). Alternatively, **Kubios threshold filter** ([Tarvainen et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2013.07.024)) flags deviations from a local median exceeding HR-scaled presets at 60 bpm (`Very Low` 0.45 s, `Low` 0.35 s, `Medium` 0.25 s, `Strong` 0.15 s, `Very Strong` 0.05 s) using cubic spline interpolation. | [Lipponen & Tarvainen, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/03091902.2019.1640306); [Tarvainen et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2013.07.024); [Berntson et al., 1990](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.1990.tb01982.x) |
| **3. Hampel Spike Filter** | BPM-only (and derived HR) | Causal sliding window (e.g., 15–30 s) computing rolling median $m_t$ and scaled $\text{MAD}_t = 1.4826 \cdot \text{median}(|x_i - m_t|)$. Flag and replace/mask sample $x_t$ if $|x_t - m_t| > k \cdot \text{MAD}_t$ ($k = 3.0$). Preserves true downward ramps and step shifts while rejecting transient motion spikes without corrupting the scale estimate. | [Hampel, 1974](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1974.10482962); [Davies & Gather, 1993](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1993.10476339); [Pearson, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/87.974338); [Leys et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.013) |
| **4. Over-Correction & SQI Gate** | Windowed features | Track fraction of corrected/interpolated beats per 60 s window. Best practice keeps total corrected RR beats **<5%** to prevent artificial suppression of RMSSD/SDNN ([Tarvainen et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2013.07.024); [Charlton et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/acead2)). If RR artifact fraction exceeds 5–10%, mark HRV features invalid for that epoch and fall back to filtered BPM; if overall valid sample coverage drops below 80%, freeze the sleep-onset accumulator. | [Charlton et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/acead2) |

## 3. Multi-Feature Sleep-Onset Detection Algorithms (BPM+RR vs. BPM-Only)

### 3.1 Empirical Benchmarks and the Quiet-Wakefulness Specificity Trap

**Key architectural insight for BPMute:** Consumer sleep trackers systematically trade away **wake specificity** to maximize overnight sleep sensitivity, routinely misclassifying motionless quiet wakefulness (reading, watching media in bed) as sleep ([de Zambotti et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000001947); [Chinoy et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsaa291)). BPMute has the **opposite cost matrix**: muting playback while the user is still awake (false positive) is highly irritating, whereas waiting an extra 2–5 minutes into stable N2 before muting (small latency bias) is benign. Detection algorithms must therefore target **high wake specificity** via multi-feature evidence, temporal persistence, and asymmetric hysteresis.

### 3.2 Feature Vector Design (Unified `BPM+RR` and `BPM-Only`)

Define features computed over causal rolling windows (updated every 10–15 s) relative to the session's robust **quiet-wake warmup baseline** $(\hat{\mu}_{\text{HR},w}, \hat{\sigma}_{\text{HR},w})$ (using median and scaled MAD, blended with personal priors once calibrated):

1. **Normalized HR drop (both modes):**
   $$z_{\text{HR}}(t) = \frac{\text{HR}_{60\text{s}}(t) - \hat{\mu}_{\text{HR},w}}{\max(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{HR},w},\; \sigma_{\min})}$$
   where $\sigma_{\min} \approx 2.0\text{–}2.5\text{ bpm}$ prevents hyper-sensitivity on ultra-quiet warmup baselines.
2. **Medium-window HR slope (both modes):** OLS or Theil-Sen slope of filtered HR over the trailing 3–5 minutes ($\text{bpm/min}$). Captures the ~−0.5 bpm/min pre-onset deceleration ramp ([de Zambotti et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz312)).
3. **Rolling HR variance ratio (both modes):**
   $$R_{\text{var}}(t) = \frac{\text{MAD}_{120\text{s}}(\text{HR}(t))}{\max(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{HR},w},\; \sigma_{\min})}$$
   Captures collapse of slow wake/sympathetic fluctuations ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006); [Walch et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz180)).
4. **Vagal dominance features (`BPM+RR` mode only, gated by SQI < 5% corrected beats):**
   - Relative vagal tone: $\Delta\ln\text{RMSSD}(t) = \ln\text{RMSSD}_{60\text{s}}(t) - \widehat{\ln\text{RMSSD}}_w$ ([Munoz et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0138921); [Plews et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8)).
   - High-frequency variability fraction: $\rho_{\text{vagal}}(t) = \text{RMSSD}_{120\text{s}}(t) / \text{SDNN}_{120\text{s}}(t)$. Because SDNN drops as VLF/LF withdraws while RMSSD stays preserved/elevated at sleep onset ([Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006)), $\rho_{\text{vagal}}$ rises cleanly without requiring noisy FFT spectral estimation.

| **1. One-Sided Lower CUSUM + EWMA** ([Page, 1954](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100); [Roberts, 1959](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1959.10489860); [Basseville & Nikiforov, 1993](#references)) | Accumulates downward shift evidence: $S_t = \max(0,\, S_{t-1} + (-z_{\text{HR}}(t) - k))$, triggering when $S_t \ge h$. Pair with EWMA smoothing of HR and variance ratio. | Mathematically optimal (minimax detection delay for a given false-alarm rate) for small persistent mean shifts (3–5 bpm); $O(1)$ memory/CPU; naturally resets to 0 on brief wake/arousal spikes; trivially interpretable parameters ($k$ slack, $h$ threshold). | Univariate by default unless applied to a composite log-likelihood score; requires clean warmup baseline $\hat{\mu}_{\text{HR},w}$. | **Excellent in both modes.** In `BPM+RR`, replace $-z_{\text{HR}}$ with a weighted multi-feature log-likelihood ratio combining $z_{\text{HR}}$, $R_{\text{var}}$, $\Delta\ln\text{RMSSD}$, and $\rho_{\text{vagal}}$. |
| **2. Forward-Filtered Hidden Markov Model (HMM)** ([Rabiner, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.18626); [Kortelainen et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITB.2010.2044797)) | Discrete latent states `{ActiveWake, QuietWake, SleepNREM}` with sticky self-transitions ($a_{ii} \approx 0.95\text{–}0.99$) and Gaussian/Student-$t$ emissions over the feature vector; runs online Forward algorithm to compute $P(S_t = \text{Sleep} \mid \mathbf{y}_{1:t})$. | **Native missing-feature marginalization:** when RR-intervals disappear (either sensor lacks RR or SQI fails), simply marginalize out the HRV dimensions from the emission likelihood—zero mode-switching discontinuity! Produces calibrated probabilities and natural hysteresis via transition matrix. | Emission means/covariances must be well-specified or anchored to per-session/per-user normalization; sensitive to outliers if Gaussian (mitigated by Student-$t$ emissions or Hampel pre-filtering). | **Best unified probabilistic architecture** for seamless `BPM+RR` ↔ `BPM-only` operation. |
| **3. Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection (BOCPD)** ([Adams & MacKay, 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)) | Maintains online posterior $P(r_t \mid x_{1:t})$ over run-length $r_t$ since the last regime switch under a hazard function $H(\tau)$. | Automatically adapts to unknown wake duration without a fixed warmup cutoff; detects variance and mean regime shifts simultaneously. | Symmetric by default (flags HR *increases* as changepoints unless constrained to negative-shift transitions); $O(t)$ growth without run-length pruning; harder to tune hazard priors for human sleep latency (5–60+ min). | Moderate; better as an offline/diagnostic baseline than a primary low-complexity daemon trigger. |
| **4. Multi-Stage Deterministic State Machine** (Wrapper) | Explicit lifecycle: `WARMUP` (5–10 min baseline capture) → `QUIET_WAKE` → `CANDIDATE` (CUSUM/HMM crosses onset threshold) → `CONFIRMED` (persists for dwell $T_{\text{confirm}}$, e.g., 3–5 min, with hysteresis reset on HR rebound/variance burst) → `GRACE_PERIOD` (cancelable soft cue/fade) → `TRIGGERED`. | Guarantees minimum warmup time, explicit dropout pausing, arousal-rebound rejection, and user-facing grace period. Prevents any single-window glitch from triggering mute. | Hand-crafted thresholds alone are brittle unless driven by standardized CUSUM or HMM posterior scores. | **Essential outer control layer** around either CUSUM or HMM. |

### 3.4 Comparing `BPM+RR` vs. `BPM-Only` Performance Tradeoffs

- **With `BPM+RR` (clean PRV available):** Combining HR deceleration + rolling HR variance collapse + preserved/rising $\ln\text{RMSSD}$ & $\rho_{\text{vagal}}$ directly measures both branches of the autonomic switch (sympathetic withdrawal **and** vagal preservation). This allows distinguishing a user lying quietly awake with slow resting HR (higher VLF/LF wander, lower RMSSD/SDNN) from consolidated N1/N2 sleep, reducing required confirmation dwell time (e.g., ~3 min) while maintaining high wake specificity ([Fonseca et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsx097); [Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006); [Altini & Kinnunen, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21134302)).
- **With `BPM-only` (no RR characteristic or high PPG jitter):** Instantaneous HR alone still carries substantial sleep-staging signal ([Sridhar et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0291-x)), and local HR standard deviation + HR drop relative to session baseline captures the primary NREM deceleration and sympathetic quieting ([Walch et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz180)). However, because beat-to-beat RSA is hidden, **specificity against motionless quiet wakefulness is lower**. Compensate explicitly in `BPM-only` mode by:
  1. Requiring a slightly stricter normalized HR drop + sustained low rolling HR variance ratio ($R_{\text{var}}$), and
  2. Extending confirmation hysteresis dwell time (e.g., 4–6 min instead of 2–3 min) before entering the cancelable grace period.
  3. Under the 3-state HMM formulation, this happens automatically: removing HRV observations widens the emission overlap between `QuietWake` and `SleepNREM`, so the forward filter requires more consecutive confirming epochs to cross the posterior threshold (e.g., $P(\text{Sleep}) \ge 0.85$).

## 4. Safe Cold-Start and Online Per-User Calibration Across Sessions

### 4.1 Why Global Absolute Thresholds Fail—and Why Naive Online Updating Drifts

Large-scale longitudinal wearable cohorts establish two foundational facts that dictate calibration architecture:

1. **Massive inter-individual spread vs. tight intra-individual stability:** Across **92,457 adults** (~33 million daily measurements), mean resting heart rate was **65 bpm** with a normal population span of **40–109 bpm** (a 70 bpm spread across healthy adults), whereas **mean within-individual daily RHR SD was only 3.03 bpm** (3.10 bpm in women, 2.90 bpm in men), with a modest seasonal rhythm peaking in January and reaching a trough in July ([Quer et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0227709)). Similarly, across **8 million individuals**, nocturnal PPG-derived RMSSD varies dramatically by age, sex, and fitness ([Natarajan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(20)30246-6)). Thus, fixed absolute thresholds (e.g., "HR < 58 bpm" or "RMSSD > 45 ms") fail catastrophically across users, yet individual baselines are stable enough to learn rapidly within 1–2 weeks.
2. **Acute outlier nights are frequent and large:** In a large free-living longitudinal study ([Altini & Plews, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21237932)):
   - **Sickness/illness** increased resting HR by **+6% on average** ($p < 0.05$, Cohen's $d = 0.97$) and reduced RMSSD by **10–12%** ($d = 0.47$).
   - **High alcohol intake** consistently elevated HR and reduced RMSSD by **12%** ($d = 0.55$).
   - **Training load and menstrual cycle** also produced measurable multi-day baseline shifts ([Altini & Plews, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21237932)).
   - About **20% of adults** experienced at least one week where RHR shifted by **≥10 bpm** ([Quer et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0227709)).
   - Naive unweighted moving averages or direct threshold updates on such nights will corrupt a user's personal priors, causing persistent false positives or missed detections for days afterward.

### 4.2 Cold-Start Strategy (Sessions 1–3)

During cold start (no user history), the detector must never compare raw BPM or raw RMSSD against a population absolute value:
- **Anchor strictly to within-session warmup baseline:** Collect 5–10 minutes of filtered quiet-wake readings at session start to estimate session $\hat{\mu}_{\text{HR},w}$, $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{HR},w}$, and (if RR available) $\widehat{\ln\text{RMSSD}}_w$.
- **Use weakly informative population priors on *relative* physiological shifts:**
  - Expected wake-to-N2 HR drop prior: $\Delta\text{HR}_0 \approx -5\%\text{ to }-7\%$ (or ~−3.5 to −5.0 bpm relative to quiet-wake baseline; [Somers et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199302043280502); [de Zambotti et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz312)).
  - Expected rolling HR variance contraction ratio prior: $R_{\text{var},0} \le 0.5\text{–}0.6$.
  - Conservative cold-start confirmation dwell (e.g., 4–5 min) + active cancelable grace period so early sessions err slightly toward late muting rather than premature false positives.

### 4.3 Empirical Bayes Conjugate Updating with Drift Guards

Once sessions accumulate, maintain a compact per-user parameter state $\theta_u = \{\mu_{\text{wake}},\, \mu_{\text{sleep}},\, \Delta\text{HR}_{\text{onset}},\, \mu_{\ln\text{RMSSD},\text{sleep}},\, \tau_{\text{score}}\}$ updated via **Normal-Normal conjugate shrinkage** ([Gelman et al., 2013](#references)) combined with **robust statistics** ([Huber, 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732); [Leys et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.013); [Plews et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8)):

1. **Shrinkage from population prior to personal posterior (early sessions):**
   $$\hat{\theta}_{n} = \frac{\kappa_0 \theta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \tilde{x}_i}{\kappa_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i}$$
   where $\kappa_0 \in [3, 5]$ is the prior pseudo-session weight, $w_i$ is the session's confidence weight (higher for clean/labeled sessions, lower for noisy sessions), and $\tilde{x}_i$ is the Huber-clipped session summary. This prevents single-night overfitting on nights 1–3 while converging smoothly within ~7–14 nights.
2. **Slow non-stationarity tracking (mature phase, $n \ge 10$):** Transition to a **capped exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA)** with effective memory ~14–30 clean sessions ($\alpha \in [0.05, 0.10]$) so the model tracks legitimate gradual changes (fitness improvement, January–July seasonal drift; [Quer et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0227709); [Plews et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8)).
3. **Outlier-Night Rejection & Huber Influence Clipping (Drift Guards):**
   - Maintain a rolling buffer of the last 14–30 clean sessions to compute personal **median** $M$ and **scaled MAD** ($S = 1.4826 \cdot \text{MAD}$; [Leys et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.013)).
   - **Pre-session anomaly detection:** If a session's warmup baseline HR exceeds $M_{\text{wake}} + 2.5 S_{\text{wake}}$ (e.g., fever/alcohol) or $\ln\text{RMSSD}$ falls below $M_{\text{HRV}} - 2.5 S_{\text{HRV}}$ (comparable to the smallest worthwhile change / acute stressor thresholds of [Plews et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8) and [Altini & Plews, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21237932)), **flag the night as anomalous**:
     - During that night, rely strictly on *within-session relative drop* rather than pulling toward the historical absolute $\mu_{\text{sleep}}$ (otherwise an elevated fever HR would prevent detection all night).
     - At session end, **quarantine** the night from updating $\theta_u$ ($w_i = 0$).
   - **Huber clipping for accepted nights:** Even for non-quarantined nights, clip residual innovations $r_i = x_i - \hat{\theta}_{n-1}$ using the Huber $\psi$-function $\psi_\delta(r_i) = \text{clip}(r_i, -\delta, +\delta)$ (e.g., $\delta = 2.0 \cdot \max(S, \sigma_{\text{floor}})$; [Huber, 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732)), bounding the maximum shift any single night can exert.
   - **Hard physiological invariants (safety rails):** Enforce hard bounds on learned personal parameters (e.g., required relative HR drop $\Delta\text{HR}_{\text{onset}} \in [-15\%, -2.5\%]$; confirmation dwell $\in [2\text{ min}, 10\text{ min}]$; decision threshold $\tau_{\text{score}} \in [\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]$).

### 4.4 Incorporating Implicit Feedback and Optional Manual Ground Truth

Different feedback channels carry radically different label quality and must receive asymmetric weights:

| Feedback Signal | Signal Meaning & Reliability | Recommended Calibration Action | Drift / Bias Safeguard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grace-period cancellation** | **High-confidence False Positive (FP):** User was definitely awake when the detector fired. | Apply bounded **Robbins-Monro** ([1951](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729586)) threshold tightening: increase $\tau_{\text{score}}$ (or CUSUM $h$) by a conservative step $+\eta_{\text{FP}}$ and/or bump confirmation dwell by +30–60 s. **Do NOT** update $\mu_{\text{sleep}}$ from this session. | Cap per-session and cumulative adjustment; if user repeatedly cancels during a single restless/insomnia night, treat as one FP event, not $k$ stacked updates. |
| **Post-trigger activity** (unmute / playback resume / input within ~2–5 min of trigger) | **Probable False Positive (FP)** (or immediate post-trigger awakening). | Same direction as grace-period cancellation with slightly smaller step ($0.5\text{–}0.75 \times \eta_{\text{FP}}$); exclude post-trigger segment from sleeping-HR estimation. | Ignore activity occurring >10–15 min post-trigger (likely normal mid-night bathroom/arousal wake, not onset FP). |
| **Uninterrupted post-trigger session** (no cancel + sustained low HR & low variance for ≥15–30 min) | **Weak/noisy Positive:** Consistent with sleep, though user could also have stopped interacting while quietly resting. | Update $\mu_{\text{sleep}}$ and $\Delta\text{HR}_{\text{onset}}$ with **moderate weight** ($w_{\text{implicit}} = 0.5$) after outlier-night check. Apply **tiny or zero** threshold loosening ($\eta_{\text{TP}} \ll \eta_{\text{FP}}$, e.g., $1:5$ ratio) so threshold slowly recovers after occasional FPs without drifting into chronic false positives. | Never loosen threshold below conservative cold-start floor without explicit ground-truth evidence. |
| **Manual watch-reported sleep onset timestamp** | **High-value labeled onset**, *but noisy* (consumer wearables exhibit systematic SOL bias and 18–60% wake specificity; [Chinoy et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsaa291); [de Zambotti et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000001947)). | Replay the stored session feature trace against the user-supplied onset timestamp $t_{\text{watch}}$: extract empirical HR drop, slope, variance ratio, and $\ln\text{RMSSD}$ at $t_{\text{watch}}$, and compare $t_{\text{detected}} - t_{\text{watch}}$. Update personal transition priors with elevated weight ($w_{\text{manual}} \approx 1.5\text{–}2.0$) rather than infinite weight. | Sanity-check $t_{\text{watch}}$ falls within recorded session bounds and shows non-artifactual HR trace; apply Huber clipping to prevent a single misreported watch timestamp from distorting $\theta_u$. |
| **Manual watch-reported average sleeping HR** | **High-value sleeping-HR anchor** (consumer wearables measure overnight mean HR accurately during stillness; [Charlton et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/acead2)). | Directly updates the user's sleeping HR prior $\mu_{\text{sleep}}$ with higher precision weight ($w_{\text{manual}} \approx 2.0$), accelerating cold-start convergence within 2–3 sessions. Note that **overnight average sleeping HR is typically 1–3 bpm lower than initial N1/N2 onset HR** because HR reaches its nadir later in the night ([Boudreau et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.3230); [Trinder et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2869.2001.00263.x)); model an explicit offset $\mu_{\text{onset}} = \mu_{\text{sleep,avg}} + \delta_{\text{nadir}}$ ($\delta_{\text{nadir}} \approx 2\text{–}3\text{ bpm}$). | Reject or Huber-clip inputs differing by $> 3 \cdot S_{\text{sleep}}$ from existing median (illness/alcohol nights) or incompatible with the session's recorded HR range. |

## 5. Recommended Reference Pipeline for BPMute

Synthesizing all four areas into a cohesive, implementable blueprint:

1. **Ingest & Contact/Quality Layer:** Parse BLE `0x2A37` flags (reject `contactStatus == 0b10`, `BPM == 0`, stale/frozen streams), unpack `1/1024` s RR intervals, apply hard physiological bounds (`[300, 2000]` ms / `[30, 200]` bpm), run causal [Lipponen & Tarvainen (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1080/03091902.2019.1640306) beat classification on RR streams and a causal 15–30 s Hampel filter ([Pearson, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/87.974338)) on BPM, and gate HRV computation when corrected RR fraction >5% ([Tarvainen et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2013.07.024)).
2. **Feature Extraction Layer (10–15 s cadence):** Compute 60 s filtered HR, 3–5 min HR slope, 120 s rolling HR MAD ratio ($R_{\text{var}}$), and—when RR SQI passes—60 s $\ln\text{RMSSD}$ and 120 s $\text{RMSSD}/\text{SDNN}$ ratio ([Munoz et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0138921); [Shinar et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2006.04.006)).
3. **Detection Engine:** Wrap a **3-state online forward-filtered HMM** (`ActiveWake` / `QuietWake` / `SleepNREM`)—or a **composite lower-CUSUM** over standardized emissions—inside a deterministic **state machine** (`WARMUP` 5–10 min → `MONITORING` → `CANDIDATE` → `CONFIRMED` with 3–5 min dwell + arousal-rebound hysteresis → `GRACE_PERIOD` → `TRIGGERED`). The HMM marginalizes HRV dimensions cleanly when running on BPM-only watches or transient RR noise.
4. **Online Calibration Store:** Persist per-session summaries and user priors $\theta_u$ updated via Empirical Bayes shrinkage ([Gelman et al., 2013](#references)) transitioning to capped EWMA, guarded by median/MAD anomaly quarantine ([Leys et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.013)), Huber innovation clipping ([Huber, 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732)), asymmetric Robbins-Monro FP tightening ([Robbins & Monro, 1951](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729586)), and bounded-weight ingestion of optional manual watch sleep onset & average sleeping HR (accounting for the onset-vs-nadir HR offset).
